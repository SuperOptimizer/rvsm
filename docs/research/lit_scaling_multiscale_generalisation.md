# Literature: scaling laws, multi-scale/scale-conditioned architectures, long-context 3D, domain generalisation

Scope: state of the art OUTSIDE the Vesuvius Challenge, relevant to usrm2 (30M-param 3D UNet, 256^3
patches, 12-rung voxel-size ladder 0.6 um - 1.2 mm, 9 coarser context cubes + scale plane + radial vector
+ cascade prediction as input, recto/verso probability output, planned fine-tune across 42 scrolls at
different voxel sizes and X-ray energies). Question behind the search: do Chinchilla-style scaling laws
apply here, how would we detect saturation, and is one unified model (vs. per-resolution/per-scroll
models) the right call. 2021-2026 preferred.

## TL;DR for usrm2

1. Nobody has published a Chinchilla-style (data, params, compute) joint power law for 3D dense
   segmentation. What exists is single-axis scaling (model size only, at roughly fixed data) on
   nnU-Net-family models, and it says **returns are positive but sub-linear and task-dependent**: easy/
   saturated tasks gain little from a bigger model, hard/large tasks (more classes, more data, thin
   structures) keep gaining out to >1B params. usrm2's task (binary surface probability, 42-scroll corpus,
   genuinely data-rich) sits on the "keeps gaining" side, so a model-size ladder experiment is worth
   running, but treat any power-law fit as a rough guide, not a budget-setting formula the way Chinchilla is
   for LLMs.
2. Detecting saturation in this literature is empirical, not derived: train a size ladder (e.g. 5M / 15M /
   30M / 60M / 120M, usrm2's own presets already span part of this) at matched data/steps, fit val-dice
   (or 1-dice, log scale) against log(params); a genuine plateau shows as the fitted exponent going to ~0
   or the curve bending over across >=3 sizes, not from one or two points.
3. usrm2's design already independently reinvents two of the strongest OUTSIDE ideas: (a) conditioning on
   voxel size/scale as an explicit input plane, which the literature converges on as one of the two working
   mechanisms for cross-resolution generalisation (the other being hypernetwork-generated weights, HyperSpace,
   MICCAI 2024); (b) multi-scale context cubes as extra input channels, which is the same family as
   cross-scale skip fusion in connectomics/EM segmentation, done because a fixed patch cannot see enough of
   a thin, curved sheet to disambiguate it. Both are corroborated externally rather than novel to us.
4. The strongest concrete recommendation from outside literature for the 42-scroll fine-tune is to add
   acquisition-metadata conditioning (energy, spacing, Paganin/phase-contrast parameters) as FiLM-style
   planes/scalars rather than only implicit normalisation — this is exactly section 21 item 3 of the design
   doc, and it is validated by domain-generalisation CT literature (metadata-conditioned + physics-aware
   augmentation beats blind pooling of scanners).
5. Global/long-range 3D context (Mamba/SSM, 3D attention) has real MICCAI-benchmark wins over plain CNNs on
   some tasks, but recent large rigorous re-benchmarks (2024-2025) show a well-tuned nnU-Net/MedNeXt CNN
   still matches or beats Mamba/Transformer variants once training budget and augmentation are controlled.
   For usrm2, the context-cube pyramid is a cheaper way to buy long-range context than replacing convolution
   with attention/SSM, and nothing found here demonstrates a documented win from >256^3 receptive fields on
   thin-structure continuity specifically outside your own domain — the closest analogue (EM/connectomics)
   gets long-range consistency from graph-based segmentation fusion across tiles, not from a bigger single
   network input.

## (a) Scaling laws / compute-optimal training for dense 3D prediction

| Method / paper | Evidence | Applicability to usrm2 | Recommendation | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **STU-Net**: Scalable and Transferable Medical Image Segmentation Models Empowered by Large-Scale Supervised Pre-training. arXiv:2304.06716 (2023), code github.com/uni-medical/STU-Net | nnU-Net-derived conv blocks made scalable; trained 14M-1.4B param variants on TotalSegmentator (~1.2k CT volumes, 104 classes) and evaluated transfer on 14 downstream sets. | **Revisiting model scaling with a U-Net benchmark for 3D medical image segmentation**, Sci Rep 2025 (also PubMed 40813440), and companion **"Scaling nnU-Net for CBCT Segmentation"**, arXiv:2411.17213 (2024) | Systematic re-benchmark of nnU-Net-family model scaling (ResEnc, ConvNeXt-style) across tasks of varying size/difficulty. | **MedNeXt: Transformer-driven Scaling of ConvNets for Medical Image Segmentation**, MICCAI 2023, arXiv:2303.09975 (v5 updated); **MedNeXt-v2**, arXiv:2512.17774 (2025) | Introduces *compound scaling* for ConvNeXt-style 3D blocks along three orthogonal axes: depth, width, kernel size — with a specific technique (UpKern) to grow kernel size by upsampling a small-kernel-trained network's weights rather than training large kernels from scratch, because training directly with large kernels on limited medical data underperforms. | **SegVol**: Universal and Interactive Volumetric Medical Image Segmentation, arXiv:2311.13385 (NeurIPS 2024) | A foundation-style 3D segmentation model scaled to 90k unlabeled + 6k labeled CT volumes, >200 anatomical categories, prompt-driven (point/box/text). Beats task-specific baselines on 19/22 tasks. Data-scale story (90k unlabeled volumes) rather than a param-count scaling law; uses a "zoom-out-zoom-in" mechanism — coarse whole-volume pass then fine local crop — for tractable inference on large volumes. | The zoom-out-zoom-in inference pattern is structurally the same idea as usrm2's top-down cascade (section 22): coarse global context informs a local crop's prediction. External validation that this pattern scales to real clinical CT foundation models, not a Vesuvius-specific trick. | No architecture change needed — usrm2 already implements the equivalent (context cubes + cascade) more cheaply than SegVol's two-pass full 3D ViT/SAM-style architecture. | **BioVFM-21M**: Benchmarking and Scaling Self-Supervised Vision Foundation Models for Biomedical Image Analysis, arXiv:2505.09329 (2025) | Studies data-scaling curves (up to 21M biomedical images) for self-supervised ViT/CNN encoders across biomedical imaging tasks (not CT-specific, not 3D-only). Reports encoder quality scaling smoothly with pretraining data size, with model-size scaling showing weaker/less consistent effect than data scaling at fixed compute in their regime. | Weak but relevant corroboration for a "data > params" prior in biomedical imaging specifically (as opposed to general vision), which matches usrm2's situation: 2.4 um scan alone is enormous (unique-window streaming, "never repeats") relative to any feasible parameter count increase. | Given usrm2's effectively unbounded unique-window data supply at rung 2, prioritise a data/step ladder (longer unique-window training) over a parameter ladder as the first experiment, then check whether a bigger model still helps once data is clearly not the bottleneck. | This paper is 2D/general biomedical imaging (not CT, not dense 3D segmentation specifically); treat as directional evidence, not a fitted law for this task. |

**On "Chinchilla-style" laws specifically**: no paper found here fits a joint `L(N, D) = ...` power law with a
derived compute-optimal `N*(C)` the way Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al. 2022, LLM-specific) does, for 3D dense
segmentation. The field instead reports scaling *trends* (bigger helps, sometimes saturates) from ablation
sweeps, not calibrated laws. Practical implication for usrm2: **treat scaling-law fitting as a local,
empirical diagnostic tool** (fit whatever ladder you actually run, on your own loss, per rung) rather than
importing a formula from outside literature — none exists yet for this problem class.

## (b) Multi-scale / scale-conditioned architectures for volumes

| Method / paper | Mechanism | Evidence | Applicability | Recommendation | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HyperSpace: Hypernetworks for spacing-adaptive image segmentation**, MICCAI 2024, arXiv:2407.03681, code github.com/ImFusionGmbH/HyperSpace | A hypernetwork takes voxel spacing as input and generates the weights (or a subset, e.g. per-layer scale/shift) of a segmentation U-Net conditioned on that spacing, instead of resampling every scan to one fixed spacing. | Reported competitive accuracy vs spacing-specific models across multiple datasets/spacings, with large inference-time flexibility (choose spacing at test time) and reduced VRAM/time at low target resolution; the extra hypernetwork cost is reported as negligible. | This is the architecturally "purer" alternative to usrm2's scale-plane approach: instead of a constant input plane telling the backbone what rung it's on, a small hypernetwork could generate per-rung FiLM parameters or even per-rung conv weights. usrm2's simpler constant-plane approach is cheaper and already implemented/working (section 2). | Treat HyperSpace as the fallback/upgrade path if the constant scale-plane conditioning is found to be insufficient (e.g. if fine-tuning across the 42-scroll corpus, which spans a much wider spacing/energy range than usrm2's own 12-rung ladder inside one scan family, shows the plane isn't expressive enough): a small hypernetwork producing FiLM (scale, shift) per decoder stage from (rung, energy, Paganin params) is a natural extension, and would let the *acquisition-metadata conditioning* of design-doc section 21 item 3 be handled uniformly with rung conditioning instead of as more constant planes. | Hypernetworks add training instability risk and a second optimisation surface; HyperSpace's own gains are "competitive with", not clearly better than, resolution-specific baselines — it buys flexibility more than accuracy. | **Voxel-spacing-agnostic training** (e.g. perivascular-space nnU-Net study, arXiv:2411.19564, and the general "no resampling, train across native spacings" pattern; **AugmentSpacing** for spacing-augmented single-model training) | Train one model across images kept at native (varying) spacing rather than resampling to a canonical grid; spacing sometimes given as augmentation, sometimes as an explicit input. | A 2026 perivascular-space study found the spacing-agnostic model "substantially outperformed" models that resampled to a fixed target spacing (compared across 0.75/0.80/0.87/1.0 mm targets). | Direct support for usrm2's core architectural bet: rather than resampling every scroll to one canonical voxel size, snap-to-nearest-rung + explicit scale conditioning should generalise better than forcing one spacing. | No change needed; cite as external validation of the ladder-plus-scale-plane design over a "resample everything to 2.4 um" alternative. | The cited study is 2D/near-isotropic MRI-range spacings (sub-mm to few-mm), a narrower spacing ratio than usrm2's 0.6 um-1.2 mm (2048x); extrapolate the *qualitative* conclusion, not the specific spacing range. |
| **Scale-Equivariant Deep Learning for 3D Data**, arXiv:2304.05864 (2023), code github.com/wimmerth/scale-equivariant-3d-convnet; **Truly Scale-Equivariant Deep Nets with Fourier Layers**, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2311.02922 | Convolution variants (steerable / Fourier-based) that are provably equivariant to input scale, so one filter bank responds consistently to a structure whether it's imaged at 1x or 2x zoom, without needing multiple resampled copies. | Demonstrated on 3D shape/volumetric benchmarks (not large-scale medical CT); scale-equivariant layers match or beat data-augmentation-only baselines for generalising across untrained scale factors. | Theoretically elegant alternative to the whole "context cube pyramid + scale plane" design: a scale-equivariant backbone could in principle absorb rung-invariance architecturally. Worth a citation as "the principled version of what we're doing pragmatically", nothing more. | These architectures are validated on small benchmarks, not on 256^3, multi-TB streaming training; adopting now would be a research risk, not an engineering win. |
| **FiLM-style acquisition/metadata conditioning** — no single canonical 3D-CT paper found combining voxel size + energy + reconstruction params as FiLM inputs, but the mechanism (FiLM: Perez et al. 2018, general) recurs across the resolution-agnostic and domain-generalisation literature surveyed here as the standard cheap conditioning tool (decoder-stage channel-wise scale/shift predicted from a small conditioning vector). | Used in various forms across HyperSpace (spacing), and cross-modality/domain-generalisation segmentation methods (see section d). | This is exactly what design-doc section 21 item 3 proposes (constant planes encoding energy, spacing, Paganin delta_beta/unsharp params, sample-detector distance, histogram percentiles). The literature doesn't add a new mechanism here so much as confirm that "encode acquisition metadata as conditioning, don't rely on implicit normalisation to erase it" is the standard move in domain-generalising across scanners. | Two encodings are both defensible and literature-supported: (1) constant planes concatenated to the input (what usrm2 already plans — simplest, no architecture change), (2) FiLM-style per-stage scale/shift from a small MLP over the metadata vector (more parameter-efficient, avoids diluting the plane's signal through many downsampling stages). Given usrm2 is already committed to concatenated constant planes for the scale channel and it works, extend the SAME mechanism to acquisition metadata for a first pass; only move to FiLM if constant planes prove to wash out for scans far outside the training distribution of energies/spacings. | Constant planes cost stem-channel width per added field; with 4-6 metadata fields plus scale plus radial plus cascade the channel count grows (already 15 channels per section 22) — watch stem parameter count and whether marginal channels get effectively ignored (checkable via input-gradient / ablation-zeroing per channel). |
| **Coordinate/positional input channels** (CoordConv-style; usrm2's own radial unit vector) | Concatenating explicit spatial coordinate channels lets a translation-invariant conv net learn position-dependent behaviour it otherwise cannot. | Well-established general result (CoordConv, Liu et al. 2018, outside the 2021-2026 window but foundational; still cited/used throughout 2021-2026 volumetric work as a standard trick, e.g. in scale-agnostic and orientation-aware segmentation papers surveyed above). | Confirms the radial-vector design choice (direction to the umbilicus) is principled, and supports design-doc section 21 item 2 (normalised radius as a further coordinate channel) and the explicit rejection of angular position/absolute z (which would break augmentation symmetry — also standard advice: only add coordinate channels compatible with your augmentation group). | Proceed with normalised-radius-from-umbilicus channel per section 21 item 2; it is a coordinate channel in the same family as the already-validated radial vector, so it needs no new evidence, only implementation and the same "no z-scoring, symmetry-permute-but-not-negate" treatment already given the scale plane and cascade channel. | None specific found beyond the augmentation-symmetry caveat usrm2 has already identified independently. |

## (e) Efficient 3D training — only flagged because it's notable

usrm2's own section 14/14b already independently discovered and measured the two biggest wins in this
category for their exact setup — the custom `up2x` gather-based upsample (replacing `F.interpolate`'s
atomic-scatter backward) and the finding that `channels_last_3d` costs more in GroupNorm+SiLU bandwidth
than it saves in cudnn NDHWC convolution throughput at their shapes, so they went back to contiguous
memory format. No outside 2021-2026 paper was found describing either specific finding for 3D medical
segmentation UNets (these read as workload-specific profiling results, not published architecture advice);
the general principles (`torch.compile`, bf16, selective activation checkpointing, avoiding unnecessary full-
batch copies) are standard, well-covered PyTorch performance practice rather than a new external result
worth citing here. **No recommendation** — usrm2's own measured, workload-specific profiling already exceeds
what generic outside literature offers for this axis; nothing found changes their section 14 conclusions.

## Overall recommendation for "does Chinchilla-style scaling apply, and how to detect saturation"

1. **No importable law exists.** Run usrm2's own size ladder (at least 3-4 sizes spanning existing presets,
   e.g. 5M/15M/30M/60M, matched steps) and fit `val_loss` (or `1 - dice`) vs `log(params)` per rung
   separately, not aggregated — per the "Revisiting model scaling" / CBCT scaling evidence that saturation is
   task/rung-dependent, not global.
2. **Data is probably not the constraint at rung 2** given the unique-window streaming design (BioVFM-21M's
   "data-scaling matters most" prior plus usrm2's own effectively-unbounded 2.4 um supply); it may well BE
   the constraint at coarse rungs (far fewer unique whole-scroll examples) and for the 42-scroll fine-tune
   (finite labelled scrolls) — so a *single* scaling conclusion across the whole ladder+corpus is the wrong
   frame; fit per-regime.
3. **Saturation detection**: a size ladder showing the fitted log-log slope flattening toward 0 across
   >=3 points, *and* the train/val gap growing with size (the CBCT-scaling paper's warning sign) rather than
   shrinking, together indicate real saturation rather than a noisy plateau from too few points or an
   undertrained largest model.
4. **One unified model is well supported** by the voxel-spacing-agnostic and scale-conditioning literature
   (section b) as the architecturally sound choice over per-resolution or per-scroll models, provided
   acquisition-metadata conditioning (section 21 item 3) is added with the same rigor already given voxel-size
   conditioning — the literature's clear message is that conditioning (constant planes, or FiLM if planes
   prove insufficient) is what makes "one model, many resolutions/scanners" work, not architecture size
   alone.
