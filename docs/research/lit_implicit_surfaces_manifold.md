# Literature survey: implicit surfaces, normals, and manifold-constrained pairs (outside Vesuvius Challenge)

Scope: state of the art *outside* the Vesuvius Challenge corpus (villa/lasagna/tsm/usrm/vc3d, already covered in
`docs/research/{villa_lasagna,villa_vesuvius,vc3d_tracer_inputs,tsm_ideas,usrm_legacy_ideas}.md` and synthesised in
`synthesis_future_inputs_outputs.md`). This note is scoped against that synthesis's Phase B (signed distance +
Eikonal, O2/O3/L5), Phase C (strict manifold recto/verso pairing, O1+O3+O4, L1/L3/L6/L7), Phase D (tracer-ready
export), and §5's top recommendations 2 and 3 (SDF head, normal head). Every citation below is checked against
what usrm2 already has evidence for (§1a/1b/1c of the synthesis) and flagged **NEW** (not in any of the five
source docs) or **CONFIRMS** (independent evidence for a synthesis claim) or **CONTRADICTS**.

Five areas, in the order asked: (a) dense SDF/UDF regression and neural implicit surfaces, (b) joint
normal/frame learning, (c) topology-preserving nested-surface (cortical) reconstruction, (d) layered-media /
unrolling, (e) multi-task head weighting.

## (a) Dense signed/unsigned distance field regression and neural implicit surfaces

| Work | Venue/Year | What it optimises | Cost | Evidence | 3D applicability | usrm2 mapping |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DeepSDF** (Park et al.) | CVPR 2019, [arXiv:1901.05103](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05103) | Per-shape latent code + MLP regresses a continuous SDF from sampled (point, distance) pairs; L1 on clamped distance | one MLP per shape class, offline sampling | canonical baseline for the whole SDF-regression line | shape-level, not volume-dense; not directly applicable to a UNet trunk | conceptual ancestor of O2/O3's clamped-distance encoding (`cap ±32`, offset 128) — usrm2 already independently converged on the same clamp-and-offset trick used here and in villa |
| **IGR — Implicit Geometric Regularization** (Gropp et al.) | ICML 2020, [arXiv:2002.10099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10099) | Eikonal term `(‖∇f‖-1)²` as a *regularizer* fit directly from a raw point cloud, no ground-truth distance needed at all | one Adam run per shape, no distance labels required | shows Eikonal alone (no distance supervision) is enough to carve a valid SDF from oriented points | point-cloud native; the "no distance label needed" property does not transfer directly to us since we already have a label source (EDT of the mask pyramid) | **CONFIRMS** L5 (Eikonal) is a real, load-bearing regularizer, not decorative — but also shows Eikonal by itself gives *no localization signal*, only shape validity. In usrm2 the Huber-on-decoded-distance term (synthesis §4 Phase B) still has to carry localization; Eikonal only keeps the gradient well-formed between samples |
| **SIREN** (Sitzmann et al.) | NeurIPS 2020, [arXiv:2006.09661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09661) | Periodic (sine) activations so first/second derivatives of the implicit field are themselves accurate, enabling direct supervision on gradient (normal) and Eikonal residual | replaces ReLU-MLP with sine-MLP, needs careful weight init | canonical for "you can supervise directly on ∇f" tasks (Poisson, Eikonal, Helmholtz) | again shape/coordinate-MLP native, not a dense-voxel decoder; usrm2's decoder is a plain conv head, not a coordinate network, so this line doesn't transfer architecturally | **pitfall for O4**: standard ReLU conv decoders (what usrm2's `w0`-parameter sdist/normal heads are) have *piecewise-constant gradients* between activation boundaries — a Scharr/central-difference normal off such a head is noisier than SIREN's analytic gradient. This is evidence *for* keeping O4 as an explicit Scharr-of-sdist derived output (synthesis §4 rec. 3) rather than ever trying to backprop through the decoder's own autograd gradient as if it were a SIREN |
| **NDF / Neural (Unsigned) Distance Fields** (Chibane et al.) | NeurIPS 2020, [arXiv:2010.13938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13938), [project](https://virtualhumans.mpi-inf.mpg.de/ndf/) | UDF (unsigned) regression specifically so **open, non-manifold surfaces** (garments, car interiors, thin sheets) can be represented — closed-surface SDF cannot | point-encoder + implicit decoder, offline distance sampling per shape | 216+ citations; established that dropping the sign entirely is the standard fix for open 2-manifolds | shape-level, but architecture-agnostic point; directly relevant conceptually because **a papyrus sheet is exactly this kind of open surface** | **CONTRADICTS the naive read, CONFIRMS usrm2's actual design**: NDF's UDF-only stance would suggest dropping the sign of O3 (`sdist`) altogether, since a single winding lamina is open and unsigned distance sidesteps the "which side is which" problem entirely. But usrm2 (and tsm, per synthesis §3.2) is *not* trying to represent one open sheet as a manifold in isolation — it needs **two coupled, oriented, locally-parallel surfaces** (recto+verso of a bounded-thickness lamina), where the sign is precisely the information that distinguishes "papyrus side" from "gap side" and gives O1's exclusivity/pairing terms (L3, Phase C) something to act on. So the synthesis's decision to keep the sign (radial-vector-derived, §2 encoding) rather than go full-UDF is correct for the *pair*, even though for a lone open sheet UDF would be the textbook answer — worth stating explicitly since a naive reading of this literature would push the wrong way |
| **NUDF: Neural Unsigned Distance Fields for high-resolution 3D medical image segmentation** | ISBI 2022 / [arXiv:2504.18344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18344), [code](https://github.com/kristineaajuhl/NUDF) | UDF regressed directly from a CT/MR volume (not a point cloud) for left-atrial-appendage segmentation; continuous field lets meshing happen at arbitrary resolution without a fixed voxel grid | trains on paired (volume, sparse surface-distance samples); inference-time sphere tracing to extract a mesh | shows UDF-from-volume (not from points) works in a real dense-CT medical setting — the closest analogue in this list to usrm2's actual input modality | **directly 3D, volume-native — most applicable single work in category (a)** | Confirms the *pattern* (dense volume in, distance field out, trained with a small conv/MLP head) usrm2 already committed to for O2/O3. Its "extract mesh only where needed, at arbitrary resolution" design is a good argument for keeping O3 continuous (not re-binarized) all the way to the tracer export (Phase D), instead of thresholding early |
| **GeoUDF** (Ren et al.) | ICCV 2023 | improves UDF gradient estimation near the zero level set (UDF gradients are undefined *on* the surface, unlike SDF) | geometry-guided normal/point projection network on top of a UDF | addresses exactly the failure mode Eikonal-on-UDF has at the surface itself | shape-level | **pitfall flag for O3/O5 if a UDF variant is ever tried**: an unsigned field's gradient is discontinuous at the zero set, so a naive Scharr-of-UDF normal (unlike Scharr-of-SDF) is unstable within about one voxel of the surface — another reason O3 should stay *signed* even though signing costs the radial-vector dependency the synthesis already flags as a pitfall (§4 Phase B, "within a configured radius of the umbilicus axis... weight 0") |
| **EikoNet / Deep Eikonal Solvers** | EikoNet: [arXiv:2004.00361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00361); Deep Eikonal Solvers: [arXiv:1903.07973](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07973) | learn a travel-time/distance field as the *solution* to the Eikonal PDE (seismic first-arrival time), via PINN-style residual minimization, no fast-marching at inference | training cost dominated by target generation at the chosen grid resolution (their own reported ballpark: ~12h/40 epochs on an RTX 3090 for a moderate 3D grid) | independent domain (seismology) converging on the same Eikonal-residual training recipe villa already ships | fully 3D, grid-based — same problem shape as O2/O3 at scale | gives usrm2 an independent cost anchor for L5: computing the Eikonal residual as a training-time regularizer (not a full grid-solve) is cheap (finite differences, no PDE solve needed at train time — same as the synthesis's own "+3-5% estimate" for L5), but *generating dense ground-truth distance targets offline* (their bottleneck) is exactly the O2/O3 "EDT per rung per store" step the synthesis already budgets as the real cost, not the loss term itself |

**Summary for (a):** the field outside Vesuvius has fully converged on "regress a clamped, signed-where-possible
distance field with an Eikonal regularizer" for exactly usrm2's reason — non-zero gradient away from the
surface, sub-voxel localization a binary mask cannot give. Nothing here contradicts synthesis recommendation 2
(O2/O3 + L5). The one genuine tension is NDF/UDF's "drop the sign for open surfaces" — resolved above: correct
advice for a *lone* sheet, wrong advice for a *paired* recto/verso where the sign is the pairing signal.

**Summary for (b):** category (b) has thin independent literature; the strongest transferable evidence is
SegRecon's "several coupled distance fields, one decoder" architecture, and cardiac fiber-mapping's demonstration
that dense orientation is learnable from volume without a hand-built structure tensor as an *output* task. Both
confirm synthesis choices already made (O4 as a shared-trunk output, O10 rejected as an input). No new technique
found here beats what's already in the plan.

## (c) Topology-preserving nested-surface (cortical) reconstruction — white/pial as a proxy for recto/verso

This is the closest available analogue to the recto/verso problem: two topologically-linked, non-intersecting,
locally-parallel 2-manifolds bounding a layer of bounded thickness (cortical gray matter / papyrus sheet body).

| Work | Venue/Year | How it keeps the two surfaces apart | Loss / mechanism | Cost | Evidence | Mapping / pitfall for Phase C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DeepCSR** | WACV 2021, [arXiv:2010.11423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11423) | predicts SDF *per surface independently* (white, pial each get their own implicit field), then a **separate topology-correction + isosurface extraction post-process** fixes any resulting intersections after the network | hypercolumn-feature MLP decoder, SDF regression at sampled continuous coordinates | training is one dense volume pass; topology correction is a non-differentiable post-process, not part of the loss | faster and more precise than FreeSurfer/FastSurfer baselines on cortical benchmarks | **key pitfall, directly relevant**: DeepCSR does *not* solve non-intersection in the loss — it accepts that independent per-surface SDF regression can still cross, and fixes it afterward algorithmically. This is a warning against assuming O3's per-surface EDT targets (synthesis §4 Phase B) will yield non-crossing recto/verso by construction; Phase C's explicit pairing/repulsion terms (L1, the thickness-band term) are doing work that DeepCSR shows a plain per-surface SDF head does *not* do for free |
| **Vox2Cortex** | MICCAI 2022, [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363906781) | explicit mesh deformation (GNN) from a template, multiple templates → multiple surfaces | Chamfer + normal-consistency + mesh-regularization losses on deformed vertices | template-mesh deformation, not a dense voxel head — heavier per-sample cost than a conv decoder | fast, widely cited | **no diffeomorphism guarantee — can still self-intersect and cross the partner surface**, explicitly noted as its main weakness in later papers. Confirms that mesh-based approaches without an explicit non-crossing constraint have the *same* problem as DeepCSR's implicit approach — the failure mode is architecture-independent, only addressed by an explicit constraint |
| **TopoFit** | 2023, [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370982335) | **jointly** reconstructs inner (white), outer (pial), *and* midthickness surfaces in one model, using their mutual interdependence as an implicit regularizer (each surface's prediction is informed by the other two) | joint mesh regression, shared features across the three surfaces | moderate; joint training of 3 correlated surfaces from shared features, not 3 independent networks | rapid, topologically-correct reconstruction reported vs. single-surface baselines | **directly actionable pattern for Phase C**: predicting the *midline* (in usrm2's terms, roughly the O6 thickness-band centerline) jointly with recto and verso, sharing features, is a cheap way to regularize pairing without an explicit sampling loss — worth considering as an alternative/complement to the "soft-sample p_v along -n" term the synthesis already proposes for Phase C's pairing term |
| **Coupled Reconstruction of Cortical Surfaces by Diffeomorphic Mesh Deformation** (a.k.a. SurfNet lineage) | NeurIPS 2023, [proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/ff0da832a110c6537e885cdfbac80a94-Abstract-Conference.html), [PMC11149912](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149912/) | estimates a **midthickness surface first**, then deforms it inward/outward via **three jointly-learned diffeomorphic flows** (mid→white, mid→pial, and a symmetric cycle loss tying them together); diffeomorphic flows are bijective by construction, which prevents self-intersection *and* the two surfaces crossing each other, with **no explicit collision-detection term needed** | symmetric cycle loss + diffeomorphic (invertible) flow parameterization; "mesh self-intersections can be prevented by diffeomorphic surface deformations without additional collision detection" | flow-ODE integration per vertex at train and inference time — non-trivial compute (explicit mesh line, not applicable to a voxel head directly) but *no* extra loss term for non-crossing, which is unusually cheap in loss-design terms even if not in compute | SOTA on ADNI/OASIS for accuracy, regularity, and speed vs. prior mesh methods | **highest-value structural idea in category (c)**: guaranteeing non-intersection by *construction* (diffeomorphism) rather than by *penalty* (repulsion loss) is a fundamentally different strategy than everything in the synthesis's Phase C (L1 repulsion, L3 exclusivity — both penalties, not constraints). It is mesh-native and does not transfer directly to usrm2's voxel-head architecture, but it is the strongest argument in this whole survey that **a penalty-based approach (what Phase C currently proposes) is the "cheap but leaky" choice, and a construction-based approach (predict thickness/midline once, derive recto/verso by a bounded, monotone offset that cannot invert) would be the "expensive but airtight" alternative** — worth a one-line note in Phase C planning, not a redesign: e.g. deriving verso as `midline − t·n` with `t` bounded away from 0 is a cheap partial version of the same idea, and is basically what O6 (thickness re-derived from the pair) already sketches |
| **CortexODE** | MICCAI 2022, [arXiv:2202.08329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08329) | learns a **diffeomorphic deformation flow via Neural ODEs**: shrink an initial surface to white matter, then expand it to pial — deformation is invertible by construction (ODE flow), so the two derived surfaces cannot cross each other or self-intersect | Neural-ODE integration (adjoint method) per vertex trajectory | ODE solves are the dominant cost — reported as fast relative to FreeSurfer but still an iterative integration at both train and inference | strong topological-correctness results, used as a building block by several follow-ups (TopoFit, Coupled Reconstruction) | same diffeomorphism-by-construction lesson as above; the "expand outward along a flow field" pattern is structurally close to what O3/O4's normal-direction offset already is, except CortexODE's flow is learned end-to-end with an explicit invertibility guarantee rather than usrm2's plan of a fixed-form `x − t·n` sample |
| **PialNN** | 2021 (MICCAI workshop) / [arXiv:2109.03693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03693) | learns a **direct one-to-one vertex mapping** from an existing white-matter surface to the pial surface (not independent reconstruction of both) | per-vertex deformation network conditioned on WM surface + local image features | cheapest of the mesh methods — single surface deformation, not joint | fast, but "may produce intersecting inner and outer surfaces... no explicit constraint to penalize intersecting generation" per later surveys — i.e. an explicitly *negative* example | mesh-native | **cautionary precedent**: PialNN's own follow-up literature calls out its lack of an explicit non-crossing constraint as its main failure mode — directly supports keeping Phase C's L1/pairing terms rather than assuming that deriving verso from recto (or vice versa) via a single offset map is automatically safe; an offset-based derivation still needs the explicit constraint unless it is literally diffeomorphic like CortexODE/Coupled-Reconstruction |

**Summary for (c), synthesized:** the cortical-surface literature has an explicit, well-evidenced split between
two strategies for keeping a nested pair of surfaces apart:
1. **Penalty-based** (independent-surface regression + a repulsion/exclusivity loss, or post-hoc topology
   correction) — this is what DeepCSR does and what usrm2's Phase C (L1, L3) already plans. Evidence: works,
   but the penalty can be violated locally (DeepCSR needs a post-process; Vox2Cortex/PialNN are explicitly
   flagged by later papers as crossing-prone without one).
2. **Construction-based** (diffeomorphic flow from a shared midline/midthickness surface, guaranteeing
   invertibility) — TopoFit, CortexODE, and the NeurIPS-2023 Coupled Reconstruction line. Evidence: SOTA
   accuracy *and* topological correctness, with the pairing/non-crossing property essentially free once the
   flow is diffeomorphic, at the cost of being a mesh-flow architecture rather than a dense per-voxel head.

For usrm2 this is the single most useful cross-check on Phase C: **the field has moved away from penalty-only
approaches toward construction-based guarantees**, and the synthesis's current Phase C plan (L1/L3/pairing-
by-sampling) is squarely in the older, "works but leaky" camp. A fully construction-based redesign (predict a
midline/thickness field and derive recto/verso by a bounded monotone offset) is out of scope for a voxel-head
architecture without adopting mesh flows, but the O6-thickness-re-derived-from-the-pair idea already in the
synthesis is a partial step in that direction and is worth strengthening rather than treating purely as a
Phase-C afterthought. Concretely: consider predicting sdist as distance to the **midline** (O3 reinterpreted)
rather than distance to recto specifically, with recto/verso derived as `midline ± (t/2)·n` where `t` is
bounded below by a minimum physical thickness — this makes crossing *structurally impossible* rather than
merely penalized, at zero extra parameter cost, and is a small, testable modification to Phase C rather than a
new phase.

## (d) Layered-media / OCT / seismic-horizon / rolled-document unrolling

| Work | Venue/Year | Mechanism | Cost | Evidence | Applicability | usrm2 mapping |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deep Relative Geologic Time (RGT)** (Bi et al.) | JGR Solid Earth 2021, [Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021JB021882) | learns a scalar field over the seismic volume that must **monotonically increase with depth**, with isosurfaces = horizons and discontinuities = faults; a single continuous field replaces per-horizon tracking | CNN over seismic cube, trained with a monotonicity-favoring regularization term plus horizon/fault supervision | established, widely used in industry; explicit claim that "small local errors propagate through monotonicity and stratigraphic coupling... producing horizon crossings" if not regularized | 3D, volumetric, dense — **structurally the closest analogue to O5 (winding phase) in the whole survey**: a single scalar field whose level sets are the sheet surfaces, with an explicit non-crossing (monotonicity) constraint | **directly informs O5/winding**: RGT is essentially "phase field for stratified media done right" — a single monotone scalar whose isosurfaces never cross by construction (enforced via the regularizer, not just hoped for). If usrm2 ever revisits O5 (currently only "geometric per-region, no ML" per synthesis §3 dead-list item 1, and unmeasured per §1b), the RGT regularization pattern — penalize `relu(-d(field)/d(depth-like-coordinate))` — is the concrete mechanism to try, and is a much better-evidenced alternative to the winding-model reverse-engineering approach the synthesis already marked dead. Caveat: RGT's "depth" axis is monotone by physical stratigraphy; usrm2's analogous coordinate (winding phase around a spiral) is *cyclic*, not monotone, so the regularizer must be adapted to a phase-unwrapping form (penalize non-monotone phase *along the radial/normal direction*, not globally) |
| **Sinusoidal-mapping RGT** (follow-up) | 2026, [arXiv:2605.01273](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01273) | maps geologic time to a periodic (sin/cos) representation specifically to handle the **wrap-around / non-uniqueness** that a raw scalar time field has at large time spans, closely paralleling why phase fields use sin/cos rather than a raw scalar | same CNN family, output reparameterized as sin/cos pair | recent (2026), addresses exactly the wraparound problem a spiral-winding phase field has | 3D, dense, cyclic-field-native | **strong direct precedent for O5's own `(sin,cos)` encoding** (synthesis O5 already specifies this): independent confirmation from an unrelated domain that a periodic scalar field over a stratified volume is best regressed as `(sin θ, cos θ)`, not raw θ, for exactly the reason usrm2 already chose it — avoids the wrap discontinuity. This is corroborating evidence usrm2's O5 design (when/if revisited) is on the right track, even though the network training itself (learned coarse winding) is the part the synthesis already killed |
| **OCT retinal layer segmentation with differentiable dynamic programming** | 2023, [PubMed 37497505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37497505/) | couples a U-Net with a **differentiable dynamic-programming surface-extraction layer** so that the *inherent layer ordering* (retinal layers always appear in the same sequence) is enforced structurally inside the network, not just as a soft loss | one extra differentiable DP layer at the end of the U-Net; no extra offline target generation | reported sub-voxel accuracy on two public OCT benchmarks, explicit claim that pixelwise labeling "does not guarantee layer topology" | 2D/2.5D (retinal B-scans) — the DP-layer trick is inherently a per-column 1D ordering constraint, which does not directly generalize to arbitrary 3D orientation the way usrm2's spiral does, but the *idea* — bake the ordering constraint into a differentiable layer rather than a loss penalty — generalizes | **NEW mechanism for Phase C's exclusivity/pairing terms**: rather than L3's `relu(p_recto+p_verso-1)` penalty (soft, can still be violated), a differentiable-DP-style approach would *structurally* force, along each ray/normal sample, recto and verso probabilities to occur in a fixed order with a minimum gap — closer to the "construction, not penalty" lesson from category (c). Costed as "one extra differentiable layer, no new offline targets," which is markedly cheaper than the mesh-flow approaches in (c); worth a concrete pilot: replace L3's elementwise penalty with a 1D soft-ordering constraint sampled along the predicted normal, which is a small extension of the pairing term the synthesis's Phase C already plans (soft-sample p_v along -n) |
| **Uncertainty-aware retinal layer segmentation via probabilistic SDFs** | 2024, [arXiv:2412.04935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04935) | represents each OCT layer boundary as a signed distance function with a per-voxel uncertainty, rather than a hard boundary label | SDF regression + uncertainty head per layer | recent (2024), targets exactly the "distance field per stratified layer boundary, plus confidence" combination synthesis's O3+O7 pair contemplates | 2.5D OCT, not full 3D | mild **CONFIRMS** for the O3+O7 pairing (signed distance + confidence head) already in the synthesis table — an independent domain reaching for the same combination |
| **Virtual/digital unrolling of rolled objects (non-Herculaneum)** | ICDAR 2024 (fully automatic X-ray-tomography unwrapping), [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-70543-4_14); DTU 3D Industry Portal case study on rolled microelectronics/flexible-PCB unrolling, [link](https://3dim-industry-portal.dtu.dk/cases-menu/visual-inspection/virtual-unrolling-for-analysing-rolled-objects); silver scroll (Jerash) CT unrolling, [Sci. Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep17765) | classical (non-learned) pipeline: segment the rolled sheet in CT, trace/parameterize the spiral surface (often by explicit layer-tracking or a fitted spiral model), resample onto a flattened 2D grid | industrial CT + segmentation + a geometric fitting/tracing step — no deep learning in the DTU or Jerash cases; the ICDAR 2024 paper is the first fully-automatic (still largely classical CV, not deep-net) pipeline found | demonstrates the *general* rolled-sheet unrolling problem is solved end-to-end for non-degraded industrial rolls (flexible PCB, silver foil) and studied for damaged/folded historical scrolls (Jerash silver scroll, Bressingham scroll) using the same tracing-and-flattening principle usrm2's downstream tracer already implements | 3D CT-native, but these pipelines assume a *segmented* sheet is already available (their hard problem is finding the spiral order and flattening, not finding the sheet in noisy CT) — exactly the boundary usrm2 sits at (usrm2 does the segmentation/SDF/normal step; the fitter does the tracing/flattening step) | **confirms the overall pipeline split** synthesis §4 Phase D already assumes (usrm2 emits `sdist`+`normal`, `fit_spiral.py` does the tracing) is the standard division of labor in this literature too, even for non-degraded industrial rolls — no evidence found that any of these methods try to do segmentation-and-unrolling as one learned end-to-end step, which is reassuring: usrm2 is not missing an established "do it all in one network" alternative, because that alternative does not exist in the literature even for easier (non-charred, non-fragmented) rolled materials |

**Summary for (d):** the strongest new material in this whole survey is here — RGT's monotonic scalar field
with an explicit non-crossing regularizer (directly reusable pattern for a hardened O5/L3), its 2026 sin/cos
follow-up (independent confirmation of O5's own encoding choice), and OCT's differentiable-ordering-layer trick
(a genuinely new, cheap mechanism for hardening L3/Phase C's exclusivity term from a penalty into a structural
constraint). The classical rolled-object unrolling literature confirms usrm2's segmentation/tracing pipeline
split is standard practice, not a gap.

## (e) Multi-task heads (segmentation + distance + normals) and loss weighting

| Work | Venue/Year | Mechanism | Cost | Evidence | usrm2 mapping |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Multi-Task Learning Using Uncertainty to Weigh Losses** (Kendall, Gal, Cipolla) | CVPR 2018, [arXiv:1705.07115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07115) | learn a per-task homoscedastic-uncertainty scalar `σ_i`, weight task `i`'s loss by `1/(2σ_i²)` plus `log σ_i`, all learned jointly with the network — no manual weight tuning | one extra scalar parameter per task, ~free | foundational, very widely used and re-derived (below) | direct alternative/upgrade to synthesis **L10** ("per-head loss balancing (EMA of per-head grad norm)... shipped OFF, never validated" in tsm). Kendall's uncertainty weighting is a different mechanism (learned log-variance vs. EMA grad-norm) with much stronger independent validation outside Vesuvius Challenge than tsm's unvalidated EMA approach — if L10 is ever revisited, prefer this formulation over porting tsm's `LossBalancer` verbatim |
| **AHU-MultiNet** (adaptive homoscedastic-uncertainty multi-task seg net, with SDF and contour auxiliary heads) | 2022, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010482522008654) | applies Kendall-style uncertainty weighting specifically to a **segmentation + signed-distance-regression + contour** multi-head setup (main seg task + two shape-prior auxiliary tasks) | same as above, applied to exactly usrm2's head composition (seg + SDF + boundary-ish) | task combination is essentially usrm2's O1(seg)+O2/O3(SDF)+O4(normal, contour-adjacent) trio, evaluated together | **directly on-point precedent for Phase B's per-channel loss dispatch** (synthesis §4: "the probability channels keep BCE+dice, the distance channel takes a Huber... the normal channels take 1-dot" — a *fixed* weighting). AHU-MultiNet's result (an SDF-regression auxiliary head reliably improves the main segmentation task when its weight is learned rather than fixed) is evidence worth testing: once O2/O3 land, an uncertainty-weighted combination of BCE+dice+Huber+normal-cosine may outperform the fixed weights currently planned, at near-zero extra cost |
| **Cross-task consistency: distance-transform decoder regularizing a mask decoder** (V-Net-based) | fragment found via search, no single canonical paper isolated — pattern appears across several 2021-2023 medical segmentation papers | a distance-transform *auxiliary decoder* is trained jointly with the segmentation decoder and an explicit consistency loss ties the two together (distance-derived mask must agree with the seg-decoder mask) | one extra decoder head + one consistency loss, no new labels beyond the same EDT already needed for the distance head | reported to sharpen boundaries and reduce false positives vs. segmentation-only baselines, repeatedly, across several groups | **CONFIRMS** the synthesis's own instinct (§4 Phase B intro: distance head attacks `offset<=3`) that distance regression, even auxiliary, tightens *localization* of a segmentation-style head — independent convergence from a literature line usrm2's own docs never cited |

**Summary for (e):** nothing here changes the plan, but two things sharpen it: (1) once O2-O4 exist, prefer
learned (Kendall-style) uncertainty weighting over tsm's never-validated EMA-based L10 if per-head balancing is
ever needed — it has an order of magnitude more independent validation; (2) the general "auxiliary distance
decoder sharpens the main segmentation boundary" result is now cross-confirmed by a distinct literature line,
which raises confidence in synthesis recommendation 2 beyond the four Vesuvius-adjacent docs it already cites.

## Net new findings not already in the five source docs

1. **Diffeomorphic-flow (construction-based) non-intersection**, from CortexODE / TopoFit / NeurIPS-2023 Coupled
   Reconstruction: guarantee non-crossing by deriving both surfaces from an invertible flow off a shared
   midline, instead of penalizing crossings after the fact. Concretely actionable at near-zero extra cost as a
   *reinterpretation* of O3 (sdist-to-midline instead of sdist-to-recto) plus a lower-bounded thickness offset
   — strengthens rather than replaces the existing O6/Phase-C pairing plan.
2. **RGT-style explicit monotonicity regularizer** for a phase/ordering field, with a 2026 sin/cos follow-up
   that independently confirms O5's own `(sin,cos)` encoding choice — a concrete, better-evidenced mechanism
   than tsm's dead learned-coarse-winding attempt, should O5 ever be revisited.
3. **Differentiable dynamic-programming / soft-ordering layer** from OCT layer segmentation: a cheap,
   structural (not merely penalized) way to enforce recto-before-verso ordering along the normal direction —
   a concrete strengthening of L3/Phase C's exclusivity term, costed at "one extra differentiable layer, no
   new labels."
4. **Kendall uncertainty-weighting**, with a directly-on-point precedent (AHU-MultiNet) using the *same*
   seg+SDF+contour head combination usrm2 is heading toward — a better-evidenced alternative to tsm's
   unvalidated L10 EMA balancer.
5. **DeepCSR / Vox2Cortex / PialNN as cautionary evidence, not just precedent**: three independent
   architectures in the closest analogous domain all needed an explicit non-crossing mechanism (post-hoc
   correction, or later papers flagging its absence as the main weakness) — corroborates that Phase C's
   pairing/exclusivity terms are necessary, not optional, and that a plain two-headed SDF/segmentation output
   (Phase B alone) will not keep recto and verso apart on its own, matching the synthesis's own phase ordering
   (Phase B before Phase C) but sharpening the reason why.

## What does not change

Nothing found here contradicts synthesis §3's "dead" list (learned coarse winding, single collapsed signed
field, feature distillation) or §5's priority order. The UDF-vs-SDF tension in category (a) resolves in favor
of usrm2's existing signed choice once the *paired* nature of recto/verso (not a lone open sheet) is accounted
for. The multi-task literature in (e) supports the synthesis's Phase B per-channel loss dispatch as designed,
with one concrete upgrade path (uncertainty weighting) if/when needed.
